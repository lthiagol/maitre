The Sous-Chef tab — the ticket

One design, and a simulator to drive it. Pick the harness, the crew, the sources, the budget and the phase on the left; the tab on the right is the tab. Every mockup here is written by render_ticket.py — the gallery is static HTML and the simulator assembles the same parts, so the two can never drift apart, and the page still reads with scripts blocked.

Fixed by the rulings — the tab is Sous-Chef, between Board and Log (Q16, Q17). Its move is it starts the automation: space spawns the crew (Q18). The rows carry the glossary's job names — orchestrator, runner, reviewer.

Everything else is on the archive — sous-chef-mockups-archived.html holds the panel, the deck, the crew, the round, the feed, the bench, the booth, the hour and the table.

The simulator

The tab is a ticket: an order at the top, what is queued under it, and a stamp at the foot saying where the work stands. Every pick below changes the tab.

What I changed in the ticket

The shape was right; six things were not. Each of these is visible in the simulator.

1 · One selection per row

The row marks what is chosen and dims what is not, the way a selected console row is marked. Before, all three crew options looked equally live, and "3" was only a number.

2 · The crew row states the consequence

"2 · runner, and the orchestrator reviews" · "1 · one agent, and the chef reviews". A lone agent cannot review itself — the close gate refuses a self-reviewed recipe — so the one-agent crew is the one where the chef holds the reviewer's chair.

3 · The queue moved inside the order

What is queued is what is being cooked. It was a separate band, as if it were another fact about the plan rather than part of the order.

4 · Policy above, progress below

The order says the budget as a policy — "3 rounds · 12 interactions". The stamp says the progress — "round 2 of 3 · 6 of 12". Two different facts, two different words, neither one repeated.

5 · The vital signs can disagree

The stamp carries the plan's domain version, this build's, and whether they agree. When they do not, the stamp says so instead of hiding it — the tab that starts the automation has to be able to report that it is not speaking the plan's rules.

6 · The footer names the move

space starts the crew; once the crew runs, the same key stops it, and the footer says which. A key whose meaning changes must say so.
